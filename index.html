Padala, Pag-IBIG and paluwagan aren’t edge cases here.

Budgie’s categories start from how money really moves in a Filipino household: family support and remittances, SSS, PhilHealth and Pag-IBIG contributions, billers like Meralco and Globe, and the cash, bank and e-wallet accounts you split it all across.

The fine print, in large print.

  • Android first

    Budgie is being built for Android phones.

  • You enter, it plans

    No bank or e-wallet linking. You log transactions yourself, and that’s what keeps your data in your hands.

  • Works offline

    Record spending without signal. It syncs when you’re back online.

  • Forecasts need a little history

    Forecasts and unusual-spending alerts start once you’ve logged enough to learn from.

  • Advice, not orders

    Budgie is decision support, not licensed financial advice. Every plan waits for your approval.

Where the numbers come from

Budgie uses actual data from the Philippine Statistics Authority’s Household Final Consumption Expenditure, which measures household spending across the Philippines. The models project spending in 12 categories, including food, housing, transport and education.

The Family Income and Expenditure Survey (FIES) supplies the 2025 spending average for families of four. Household Final Consumption Expenditure (HFCE) supplies the national trends used in the projections.

The household average

The 2025 FIES average for a family of 4 is ₱339,421 per year, or ₱28,285 per month when divided by 12. It uses only 29,346 records with average family size (FSIZE) exactly 4, weighting total expenditure (TOTEX) by the family / household weight (RFACT). This is an annual survey average expressed monthly, not an observed monthly series or a 2027 forecast. Total expenditure includes non-cash items such as imputed rent.

How to read the charts

The peso amounts illustrate the 2027 national seasonal pattern scaled so the 12 monthly amounts average the 2025 FIES family-of-four baseline. The middle line is ₱28,285; bars extend above or below that average. Labels use thousands of pesos: ₱28.3k is about ₱28,300. The percentages compare consecutive monthly amounts, with January compared against the preceding December on the same scale. These amounts illustrate seasonality at a fixed average; they are not observed monthly FIES data, an inflation adjustment, or a personal forecast.

The models forecast by quarter. To draw a monthly chart, we spread each quarter’s projected growth evenly over its three months. The monthly points are estimates derived from those quarterly forecasts.
